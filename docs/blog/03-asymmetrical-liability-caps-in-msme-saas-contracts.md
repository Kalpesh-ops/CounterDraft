# Unpacking Asymmetrical Liability Caps in Commercial and SaaS Contracts

*By CounterDraft Legal Research Group*

---

### The Hidden Trap in Modern Standard-Form Vendor Agreements

When an MSME, startup, or corporate buyer subscribes to cloud software, managed IT services, or marketing consultancy, the commercial contract often features an extensive, dense "Limitation of Liability" clause:

> *"In no event shall Vendor cumulative aggregate liability arising out of or related to this Agreement, under any legal theory, exceed the total fees actually paid by Customer in the one (1) month preceding the incident giving rise to liability."*

Simultaneously, the "Customer Indemnification" clause across the page demands:

> *"Customer shall defend, indemnify, and hold harmless Vendor against all third-party claims, liabilities, judgments, and legal expenses without monetary limit."*

This represents a classic **asymmetric liability architecture**:
- If the Customer inadvertently causes an issue, their liability is **uncapped**.
- If the Vendor suffers a catastrophic ransomware breach, exposes confidential customer data, or wipes out business databases, the Customer's maximum legal recovery is capped at **a single month of subscription fees** (e.g. Rs. 15,000).

---

### The Doctrine of Unconscionability: *Central Inland Water Transport* (1986)

In Indian jurisprudence, unconscionable standard adhesion contracts are subject to direct constitutional and statutory scrutiny under Section 23 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872.

In ***Central Inland Water Transport Corp v. Brojo Nath Ganguly (1986) 3 SCC 156***, the Supreme Court of India laid down the doctrine of contractual fairness:
- **Unequal Bargaining Power**: Where one party possesses overwhelming commercial dominance and presents standard "take-it-or-leave-it" terms to a weaker counterparty who has no meaningful choice, the resulting contract is not a true voluntary bargain.
- **Opposed to Public Policy**: Provisions that shock the judicial conscience, completely eliminate accountability for gross neglect, or create unconscionable asymmetries are void as opposed to public policy under Section 23.
- **Judicial Intervention**: Indian courts have the inherent equitable jurisdiction to refuse enforcement of unfair terms and interpret clauses against the party that drafted them (*contra proferentem*).

### Key Carve-Outs Every Buyer Must Insist Upon

A standard limitation of liability clause is not inherently illegitimate; commercial enterprises require predictable risk boundaries. However, a liability cap becomes dangerous when it fails to include standard commercial **"Super-Caps" or Carve-Outs**.

Any commercially viable agreement must exclude the following from the liability cap:
1. **Breach of Confidentiality**: If proprietary trade secrets or customer lists are leaked, damages far exceed subscription fees.
2. **Breach of Data Protection & Privacy**: In light of India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDPA), 2023, data fiduciary obligations and regulatory penalties cannot be shielded behind a 1-month fee cap.
3. **Gross Negligence and Willful Misconduct**: A vendor cannot contractually immunize itself against deliberate disregard for cybersecurity protocols.
4. **Third-Party Intellectual Property Infringement**: If the vendor's software infringes another company's patent or copyright, the vendor must fully indemnify the customer without a monetary ceiling.

---

### Recommended Redline for Commercial Negotiations

When counter-drafting an enterprise service agreement or software contract, submit the following balanced revision:

> **Proposed Counter-Proposal**:  
> *"Except for breaches of Section [X] (Confidentiality), Section [Y] (Data Privacy & Security), and liabilities arising from gross negligence or willful misconduct (which shall remain uncapped), each party's aggregate cumulative liability under this Agreement shall be capped at the total fees paid or payable by Customer in the preceding twelve (12) months. Furthermore, Vendor shall provide reciprocal indemnification protecting Customer against third-party intellectual property infringement claims."*
