# Lease Forfeiture Pitfalls: How Section 74 & Section 108 Protect Tenant Security Deposits

*By CounterDraft Legal Research Group*

---

### The Reality of Tenancy Security Deposit Disputes

Across residential and commercial leasing in urban centers like Bengaluru, Mumbai, Delhi-NCR, and Hyderabad, security deposit disputes are among the most frequent contractual conflicts.

A tenant provides three to ten months of rent upfront as an interest-free security deposit. Upon serving notice or vacating at the end of the term, the landlord suddenly declares:
- The entire security deposit is forfeited due to early departure.
- Heavier-than-expected "painting and repainting" fees equivalent to an entire month's rent are deducted without invoices.
- Unpaid compounding daily late fees (e.g. Rs. 500 per day of rent delay) are retroactively withheld from the deposit.

To navigate this imbalance, tenants must understand their statutory safeguards under the **Indian Contract Act, 1872** and the **Transfer of Property Act, 1882**.

---

### Section 74: Liquidated Damages vs. Illegal Penalties

Contract drafters frequently believe that writing *"Landlord shall be entitled to forfeit the entire security deposit as liquidated damages upon breach"* makes forfeiture automatic.

This belief violates Section 74 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, as interpreted by the Supreme Court of India in the landmark ruling ***Kailash Nath Associates v. Delhi Development Authority (2015) 4 SCC 136***:

1. **Proof of Actual Financial Injury is Mandatory**: The Supreme Court held that liquidated damages cannot be claimed simply because a clause mentions a fixed sum. The complaining party must prove that they actually suffered monetary loss as a direct consequence of the breach.
2. **Forfeiture as Penalty**: If a tenant vacates after giving reasonable notice, and the landlord promptly re-lets the apartment to a new tenant at equal or higher rent, the landlord has suffered zero financial loss. Under Section 74, retaining the tenant's multi-lakh deposit constitutes an **illegal penal forfeiture**.
3. **Reasonable Compensation Only**: Even where a breach occurs, the court will award only reasonable compensation strictly commensurate with proven losses, not an arbitrary windfall.

---

### Section 108(c) & (m): Quiet Enjoyment & Wear-and-Tear Carve-Outs

The Transfer of Property Act, 1882 establishes fundamental rights that standard lease agreements often attempt to waive:

- **Section 108(c) - Right to Quiet Enjoyment**: The lessor is statutorily presumed to covenant that the lessee shall hold uninterrupted possession of the property throughout the term. Landlords cannot arbitrarily enter the premises, shut off water or electrical connections, or disrupt tenancy without notice.
- **Section 108(m) - Reasonable Wear and Tear**: The law requires the tenant to restore the premises in good condition, **"reasonable wear and tear excepted"**. Ordinary fading of wall paint, minor scuffs from hanging art, or gradual weathering from climate cannot be billed to the tenant as "compensable damage."

---

### Compounding Daily Late Fees: When Does a Charge Become a Penalty?

Many standard rental agreements mandate exorbitant late fees: *"Tenant shall pay a late fee of Rs. 500 per day for each day rent remains unpaid beyond the 5th of the month."*

On a monthly rent of Rs. 30,000, a Rs. 500/day charge represents a compounding penalty of **50% to 60% per annum**—far exceeding statutory and commercial interest benchmarks. Indian courts routinely strike down compounding per-day fees as coercive penalties under Section 74, restricting recovery to standard commercial bank interest (typically 12% to 18% per annum pro-rata).

### Actionable Counter-Drafting Safeguards

Before executing a residential or commercial lease, propose these two essential amendments:

#### 1. Security Deposit Refund & Damage Audit Clause
> *"Landlord shall hold the Security Deposit in trust and shall refund the entire deposit to Tenant within fourteen (14) calendar days following physical handover of keys. Deductions from the deposit shall be strictly restricted to verified unpaid utility dues and substantiated physical damages beyond ordinary wear and tear, documented by itemized receipts and a signed joint move-out inspection log."*

#### 2. Reciprocal Termination & Cure Notice
> *"Either party may terminate this agreement upon providing thirty (30) days prior written notice. Prior to declaring default or initiating deductions, Landlord shall provide Tenant with a written notice specifying the alleged breach and granting a minimum of fourteen (14) days cure period."*
