# Statutory Reference Handbook & Judicial Precedent Digest

> Comprehensive legal digest of Indian statutory provisions and appellate authorities referenced throughout CounterDraft.

---

## 1. Indian Contract Act, 1872 (Act No. 9 of 1872)

### Section 10: What agreements are contracts
- **Statutory Text**: All agreements are contracts if they are made by the free consent of parties competent to contract, for a lawful consideration and with a lawful object, and are not hereby expressly declared to be void.
- **Application in CounterDraft**: Foundational requirement for mutual assent and voluntariness. Where standard contracts are signed under economic duress or lack meaningful choice, the enforceability of one-sided waivers is scrutinized.

### Section 23: What considerations and objects are lawful
- **Statutory Text**: The consideration or object of an agreement is lawful, unless it is forbidden by law, or is of such a nature that, if permitted, it would defeat the provisions of any law; or is fraudulent... or the Court regards it as immoral, or opposed to public policy.
- **Application in CounterDraft**: Grounds the defense against unconscionable clauses, arbitrary employer termination terms, and total waivers of liability.

### Section 27: Agreement in restraint of trade, void
- **Statutory Text**: Every agreement by which any one is restrained from exercising a lawful profession, trade or business of any kind, is to that extent void.
- **Exception**: Saving of agreement not to carry on business of which good-will is sold.
- **Application in CounterDraft**: Automatic invalidation of post-departure employment non-compete clauses, non-solicitation covenants operating as disguised non-competes, and territorial bans on professional consulting.

### Section 73: Compensation for loss or damage caused by breach of contract
- **Statutory Text**: When a contract has been broken, the party who suffers by such breach is entitled to receive, from the party who has broken the contract, compensation for any loss or damage caused to him thereby, which naturally arose in the usual course of things from such breach, or which the parties knew, when they made the contract, to be likely to result from the breach of it.
- **Rule on Remoteness**: Such compensation is not to be given for any remote and indirect loss or damage sustained by reason of the breach.
- **Application in CounterDraft**: Establishes that damage claims must be tied to direct, foreseeable losses rather than punitive multiplier damages.

### Section 74: Compensation for breach of contract where penalty stipulated for
- **Statutory Text**: When a contract has been broken, if a sum is named in the contract as the amount to be paid in case of such breach, or if the contract contains any other stipulation by way of penalty, the party complaining of the breach is entitled, whether or not actual damage or loss is proved to have been caused thereby, to receive from the party who has broken the contract reasonable compensation not exceeding the amount so named or, as the case may be, the penalty stipulated for.
- **Judicial Construction**: Despite the words "whether or not actual damage... is proved", the Supreme Court (*Kailash Nath*) held that proof of actual injury remains a prerequisite wherever damage is capable of commercial assessment.
- **Application in CounterDraft**: Invalidates unilateral security deposit confiscation and disproportionate delay penalties.

### Sections 124 & 125: Contracts of Indemnity
- **Statutory Text**: Section 124 defines a contract of indemnity as an agreement to save the promisee from loss caused to him by the conduct of the promisor or by the conduct of any other person. Section 125 details rights of the indemnity-holder.
- **Application in CounterDraft**: Mandates that indemnities must be reciprocal, capped to direct losses, and accompanied by immediate notice and defense rights.

## 2. Transfer of Property Act, 1882 (Act No. 4 of 1882)

### Section 108: Rights and liabilities of lessor and lessee
- **Clause (c) - Quiet Enjoyment**: The lessor shall be deemed to contract with the lessee that, if the latter pays the rent and performs contracts binding on him, he may hold the property during the time specified without interruption.
- **Clause (f) - Repairs by Lessee**: If the lessor neglects to make any repairs which he is bound to make, the lessee may make them and deduct the expense from the rent.
- **Clause (m) - Maintenance**: The lessee is bound to keep, and on the termination of the lease to restore, the property in as good condition as it was at the time he was put in possession, reasonable wear and tear excepted.
- **Application in CounterDraft**: Used to counter one-sided lease covenants forcing tenants to pay for external building repairs, dampness remediation, or structural cracks, and preserving statutory rights to uninterrupted possession.

## 3. Landmark Judicial Authorities Digest

### 1. Percept D'Mark (India) Pvt. Ltd. v. Zaheer Khan & Anr.
- **Citation**: (2006) 4 SCC 227
- **Bench**: Supreme Court of India (Ar. Lakshmanan & L.S. Panta, JJ.)
- **Key Proposition**: Under Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, a restrictive covenant extending beyond the term of the agreement is void ab initio. The doctrine of restraint of trade applies unreservedly to post-termination periods. The English doctrine of reasonableness of restraint does not apply in India.

### 2. Kailash Nath Associates v. Delhi Development Authority
- **Citation**: (2015) 4 SCC 136
- **Bench**: Supreme Court of India (Ranjan Gogoi & R.F. Nariman, JJ.)
- **Key Proposition**: Forfeiture of earnest money or security deposits is subject to Section 74 of the Contract Act. Liquidated damages can only be awarded when actual loss is suffered and proved. Where no financial injury is established, forfeiture is treated as an illegal penal forfeiture.

### 3. Central Inland Water Transport Corp Ltd. v. Brojo Nath Ganguly
- **Citation**: (1986) 3 SCC 156
- **Bench**: Supreme Court of India (V.D. Tulzapurkar, D.P. Madon, R.S. Pathak, JJ.)
- **Key Proposition**: Contracts that are grossly unfair, unequal, and opposed to public policy under Section 23 cannot be enforced. Standard form contracts signed by weaker economic parties without bargaining equality are subject to judicial invalidation under principles of justice, equity, and good conscience.

### 4. Niranjan Shankar Golikari v. The Century Spinning & Mfg. Co. Ltd.
- **Citation**: (1967) 2 SCR 378
- **Bench**: Supreme Court of India (J.M. Shelat & V. Bhargava, JJ.)
- **Key Proposition**: A covenant restraining an employee from engaging in competing employment *during the active tenure* of their employment agreement is valid and does not violate Section 27. Restraints during the subsistence of employment serve to ensure devotion of service, distinct from post-employment restrictions.

### 5. LIC of India v. Consumer Education & Research Centre
- **Citation**: (1995) 5 SCC 482
- **Bench**: Supreme Court of India (K. Ramaswamy & B.L. Hansaria, JJ.)
- **Key Proposition**: In standard-form adhesion contracts, terms must meet the test of reasonableness and fairness. Public and corporate institutions cannot impose arbitrary conditions on the public.

### 6. Vidya Drolia & Ors. v. Durga Trading Corporation
- **Citation**: (2021) 2 SCC 1
- **Bench**: Supreme Court of India (N.V. Ramana, Sanjiv Khanna, Krishna Murari, JJ.)
- **Key Proposition**: Established the four-fold test for arbitrability. Landlord-tenant disputes governed by specialized state rent control legislation remain exclusively within the jurisdiction of statutory rent tribunals and are non-arbitrable.
